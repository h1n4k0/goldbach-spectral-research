# Independent audit: sparse-support Beta-block exception estimate

**Audit outcome:** the conditional exception estimate and its stated limitations check. This records an internal mathematical audit, not external peer review or a priority assertion. Checked proof file: `arithmetic-post-round10-exceptions.md`, SHA-256 `8ef68e4b83b83fde4bfec4abb56e689013f81643f1e8de8eafd4587e7d3269ce`. Baseline: [Round 10 at its pinned release](https://github.com/h1n4k0/goldbach-spectral-research/blob/df49c707d35986232009cff619b9b23cdaa4dad3/arithmetic-round10.md). Below, $X=8R^2$ so all raw intervals can lie in $[0,X]$.

1. **Selected support is preserved.** For any subset $E$ with $M=|E|$ and bounded signed weights, the fourth moment of its doubled-square exponential sum is bounded by the energy of differences $r^2-u^2$. The zero difference has multiplicity exactly $M$; any nonzero difference has at most $d(|t|)$ representations by $(r-u)(r+u)=t$, and the total mass of all multiplicities is $M^2$. This gives $O_\epsilon(M^{1/2}X^\epsilon)$ for the fourth norm. By [Montgomery–Vaughan, Theorem 17.1](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/571s25/montgomery-vaughanII.pdf#page=77), the product of the two prime sums has minor-arc $4/3$ norm $O(X^{5/4}P^{-1/4}L^{11/4})$ (interpolation exponents $3/4$ on the $L^1$ mass and $1/4$ on the sup norm). The proof therefore replaces the old full-dyad $\sqrt R$ factor by $\sqrt M$, not merely an averaged one-dimensional sign cancellation.
2. **Major errors and tails are individual.** Finite character Parseval makes the normalized absolute Gauss-coefficient sum at most $\sqrt q$. On the arc $|\beta|\le P/(qX)$, the GRH prime error is $O(\sqrt{Xq}L^2(1+X|\beta|))$; the continuous interval bound is $O(X/(1+X|\beta|))$. Summing the mixed product errors over reduced fractions and integrating with $t=X\beta$ gives $O(\sqrt X P^{3/2}L^2)$, while the two-error product gives $O(P^3L^5)$. This precedes summing over $r\in E$. The absolute Ramanujan tail bounds are uniform in every qualifying $s$: equivalently expand $|c_q(s)|\le (q,s)=\sum_{d\mid q,d\mid s}\varphi(d)$ and sum the divisor split separately for $q>P$ and weighted $q\le P$. Extending the beta integral costs $O(qX/P)$ per coefficient. At $P=X^{1/3-\delta}$ these losses are $O_\delta(X^{1-\delta})$ individually after choosing the auxiliary exponent small. GRH here is needed for the auxiliary $q\le P$ character estimates, and cannot be reduced to local GRH at the tested $2r^2$ moduli.
3. **The window and threshold can be chosen in the asserted order.** Positivity brackets each moving ratio box on a slice by two fixed boxes. Throughout a slice the continuous difference is $O(X/K)$, and the total selected square-root weight of the slices is at most $\sqrt{KM}$. Reimposing units loses $O(X^{-1/2}L^2)$ per normalized count. The positive $\sqrt{uv}$ layer cake and integration over the fixed compact $J^2$ preserve the estimate for arbitrary $E$; the sieve sup bound permits an $L^1$ signed-discrepancy estimate to bound the squared kernel. This holds before choosing $E$ by the full HS compression. For $K=\lceil L^3\tau^{-2}\rceil$, $\tau\ge X^{-\delta/4}$, the terms $M(LX^{-\delta}+L^2/K)$ are $o(M\tau^2)$, and $K\ll R$. Squaring the surviving $\sqrt M$ term yields the stated $\tau^{-6}X^{1/3+\delta/2+\epsilon}$, after absorbing logs and relabeling the arbitrary small exponent. In particular fixed inverse-log thresholds allow $X^{1/3+\epsilon}$.

**Novelty and limits.** This improves the pinned project's $X^{199/400}$ Markov exponent, for the compensated HS compression of this one fixed block and under the stated global GRH. It is not an improvement claim for the number of integers lacking any Goldbach representation; compare [Althöfer–Chen–Perelli–Pintz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09386) for that different question. It supplies neither an all-square estimate nor a uniform bound on the full operator, and proves or disproves neither S nor R.
