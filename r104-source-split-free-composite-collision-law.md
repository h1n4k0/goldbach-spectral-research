# The split-free composite carrier and a nonzero canonical collision term

Keep the [actual canonical rows](r103-source-canonical-size-free-real-upper-factor.md), the old prime $s$ with $5T/4\le\log s\le7T/4$, the hard physical window $\mathcal J$, true external floors in a fixed finite box $0\le a,b\le A_0$, actual squarefree $N$-units and every literal long/product test. As before $L=\log X$, $\ell=\log L$, $T=L/\ell$. Write each cofactor as $h=sV$ or $k=s'V'$. Require $V,V'>1$, squarefree, with each of their primes at least $e^{4T}$, but impose no condition on the largest of those primes. In particular there is no split parameter or upper-prime gate. The true distinguished prime is $F=P^+(n)$ and similarly $G=P^+(m)$. The excluded case $h=s$ is **not** included: its complete containing orbit need not vanish. Write $W_j^{\mathrm{comp},\mathbf u}$ for the resulting genuine Walsh rows, with the [same unnormalized convention](r96-source-walsh-two-full-and-dominance-switch.md).

Let $b_\xi^+(v)$ be the real density off zero of the positive reciprocal convolution exponential with kernel $\mathbf1_{\xi<u\le .7}du/u$. Use the exact new normalizer

$$
 \mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{comp}}
    =\frac{|\mathcal J|}{\ell}\sum_{s\ {\rm old}}
                        \frac{b^+_{4/\ell}(1/2-\log s/L)}s,
 \qquad
 \mathcal N_N^{\mathrm{comp}}
    =\Sigma(N)B_{\mathcal J}(N)
                   (\mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{comp}}/|\mathcal J|)^2. \tag{1}
$$

**Theorem.** Uniformly $\mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{comp}}\asymp X$. Keep the original seven phases $A_j(a,b)$ and intrinsic bits $\mathbf b_j$. Define the error by subtracting exactly $\mathbf1_{\mathbf u=\mathbf b_j}\mathcal N_N^{\mathrm{comp}}A_j(a,b)$ from the arithmetic row. For $m=m_j(\mathbf u)$ surviving full signs use $w_0=w_1=1,w_2=2$. At any fixed common floors the squared norm of the maximum error over the finite rows and bit choices with the indicated $m$ is $O(X^3\ell^{-2w_m})$. With $r\in\{1,2\}$ independently target-adaptive floor axes, the squared norm of the common maximum is $O(X^3\ell^{-2w_m/(r+1)})$, the other floors being common and fixed.

In addition, if the box includes $a=b=41/2$, there is a nonzero **actual** two-full-sign asymptotic in the double-nonlong row $D_n$. With the continuous Dickman function $\rho$, set

$$
 \mathfrak c
   =2\int_{20}^{41/2}\int_{5/4}^{7/4}
           \rho\!\left(\frac{2u-t}{4}\right)\frac{dt}{t}\,du
   >0 .
                                                                    \tag{2}
$$

Then at those fixed floors the true coefficient has the norm asymptotic

$$
 \left\| W_{D_n}^{\mathrm{comp},(1,1)}
                +\frac{\mathfrak c^2}{\ell^2}
                         \Sigma(N)B_{\mathcal J}(N)\right\|_{\ell^2(N)}
                \ll X^{3/2}\ell^{-3},                               \tag{3}
$$

where $N$ ranges over the actual even targets. Its squared norm is thus of order $X^3\ell^{-4}$. On a fixed interior target subwindow it is strictly negative with magnitude of order at least $X/\ell^2$ off $O(X/\ell^2)$ even targets. This is a sign and sharp scale for the named actual Walsh row only, not for the full pair sum, an exceptional modulus, or Hypothesis S.

## 1. Remove the upper-prime gate, not the composite condition

Because $h,k$ are explicitly squarefree composite, their complete containing orbits vanish by $\Lambda(h)=\Lambda(k)=0$. Thus the [capped-orbit calculation](r85-source-capped-long-floor-orbits.md), (4)–(5), applies before making any restriction on the physical size of a cofactor. It gives the same seven actual row formulas and signs. Only after that calculation use the positive bounds $\log h,\log k=L/2+O_{A_0}(T)$ and the fixed finite cap $M=\exp((24+2A_0)T)$ on the short labels, by the same divisor and prime-floor geometry. In particular $h=s$ cannot be inserted by reasoning from these *post-cancellation* windows. The separated rooted comparison used below is only a device for the expanded rows.

At a fixed old prime and short label, fix all primes in the short block $[e^{4T},e^{AT}]$ for fixed $A>24+2A_0$, and write $\xi=A/\ell$. The real reciprocal product of the remaining prime factors now uses the complete interval $\xi<u\le .7$. With the exact factor sign, its real representative restricted to the active compact range is the **single** signed Dickman measure $A_\xi$ of the [full-sign calculation](r98-source-tag-full-sign-stretched-cancellation.md), §3. The composite condition removes a unit atom for the empty product of *all* cofactor primes beyond $s$; its support is not in the active log cofactor range. A unit atom for the remaining factors when the fixed low block is nonempty can be accounted for separately as below. No subtraction of a smooth upper-prime chamber is involved.

The same reciprocal tuple replacement of the [size-free upper-gate proof](r103-source-canonical-size-free-real-upper-factor.md), §§1–2, applies on hard intervals and bounded-variation marks: total reciprocal mass is $O(\log\ell)$, the least prime here is at least $e^{AT}$, and the summed distinct-prime discrepancy is arbitrary inverse-logarithmic. For the short block, its positive labeled reciprocal mass is bounded, its expected logarithmic displacement is $O(T)$, and its exponential moment for the displacement divided by $AT$ is bounded, exactly as in that proof. Discard the far event where that displacement is greater than $.05L$ in the *density* comparison by this last bound. The complete positive density off zero is everywhere $O(\xi^{-1})$; on the compact window it is Lipschitz of the same size, by the coordinate marking identity. For the far event involving the *atom*, at fixed low block and old prime its entire size in a physical one-arm density is at most its reciprocal mass, with bounded prime mark. The total reciprocal mass in this far event is $e^{-c\ell}$ even after every allowed label is counted. Thus the atom contributes a negligible density and total variation; each clipped physical interval has bounded variation independently of that block, including both endpoint jumps. This argument does not discard any actual integer term.

On the remaining compact log arguments, the density of $A_\xi$ is $-\rho(v/\xi-1)/v$, smaller than $\ell^{-B}$ for every fixed $B$ by factorial decay. Hence the rooted full-sign fourth norm on a bin of normalized width $\delta=\ell^{-3}$ is $O_B(X^{3/4}\delta\ell^{-B})$; the positive fourth norm is $O(X^{3/4}\delta)$. This also holds unbinned for a fixed full near-square-root prime interval constrained only by a fixed finite exponent box. To justify the variation bounds, the common part of a fixed-label cofactor window uses the same bounded scaled derivative of the bare prime mark as before; clip-endpoint motion costs positively $O(\ell/L)$, smaller than the stated powers. In the far-block atom calculation the cutoff in the physical variable is a fixed interval or fixed finite intersection of intervals for the fixed cofactor, so its variation costs at most a constant uniformly in all labels and shifts. The Siegel–Walfisz and hard half-integer Perron minor argument is unchanged: complete alphabets still have at most two candidate roots and only $\ell^{O(1)}$ short labels in the active windows, and the discrete replacement errors can be taken to an arbitrarily high log power before integrating the auxiliary arcs.

Restore the removed largest and prime-repeat vetoes on each bin arm, as in the [complete-alphabet collision argument](r102-source-canonical-full-alphabets-size-gate.md). It does not need an upper-prime condition: any rejected root has another prime factor at least as large as itself, counting a repeat, leaving all other factors in a fixed log-ratio band. The near-square-root cofactor prime is unique, with reciprocal real density $1/v=O(1)$ in the compact range; the labeled reciprocal mass of the other small factors is bounded. The rejected signed bin is thus bounded in fourth norm by $O(X^{3/4}\delta/\ell)$, and the unbinned correction is $O(X^{3/4}/\ell)$. Subtract on each **arm**, before multiplying its signed bound by that of its mate. The resulting true signed arm has norm $O(X^{3/4}\delta/\ell)$, and the accepted pair products give the claimed fixed zero-comparator norms. The $O(\delta^{-1})$ ambiguous pairs keep their literal tests and contribute $O(X^{3/2}\delta)$ positively, smaller than either signed norm. Old true unit and prime-floor restoration estimates are negligible. All hard-bin reasoning is in the [two genuine label coordinates](r89-source-four-actual-parity-phases.md); root comparison has not added a new divisor boundary.

In the matching channel use the same signed short-label scalar and the seven separate continuum shapes as in the size-free upper-gate proof. The only new upper density is $b_\xi^+$ without a chamber subtraction. Its compact positive bound and Lipschitz bound give the same density and variation error $O(\delta/\ell+1/T)$ per bin, so the rational completion and bin summation cost $O(X^{3/2}/\ell)$. For fixed old prime the low logarithmic displacement averages $O(T)$, costing $O(\ell^{-1})$ at normalized scalar scale; the negligible far block, including its possible atom, was handled above. The actual short-label Euler measure is unchanged. For the detached positive factor compare it to (1) by convolving with the positive small-block kernel from $4/\ell$ to $\xi$; its mass is $A/4$ and its expected displacement is $O(\ell^{-1})$. The corresponding discrete factor has the same mass to arbitrary inverse-logarithmic precision by reciprocal PNT. This is the same matching calibration as before, not a normalization chosen from the clipped row. After multiplication by $\Sigma(N)B_{\mathcal J}(N)$, restore canonical roots at the unbinned positive error $O(X^{3/2}/\ell)$. This proves the matching comparison with (1).

The upper bound on (1) follows from the positive density. For its lower bound, use the fixed positive-length reference prime-exponent interval of the size-free upper-gate proof. Its exact rooted first moment equals its interval length times (1)'s $\mathcal C_X^{\mathrm{comp}}$ with error $O(X/\ell)$, by the same compact replacement. The first moment contains the corresponding rooted terms for, say, the old fixed split $Z_0$ with the upper-prime gate; there the genuine positive normalization is uniformly $\gg X$ and has the same calibration. This proves the new lower bound by actual inclusion of weighted integer terms. No componentwise comparison of different real normalizers is required.

Only the floors can move. A true change is bounded by the independent positive prime-slab envelopes of the [seven-row proof](r95-source-off-axis-right-forbidden-dominance.md), with the complete unsigned arm in the unchanged leg. The same root multiplicity and positive density bounds prove the target norm $O(X^{3/2}(d+\ell/L))$ for floor width $d$, independently of the other floor. In matching the normalization no longer moves, and the phases are Lipschitz. With $r$ free floors at spacing $\eta$, summed squared one-axis gap costs are $O(X^3\eta)$ without a grid factor and nodal costs are $O(X^3\eta^{-r}\ell^{-2w_m})$. Choose $\eta=\ell^{-2w_m/(r+1)}$. This proves the stated common maxima on the actual support.

For example, fix the old highest cut $Z=X^{\delta_0}$. Choose a fixed integer $k_0$ with $1/(2k_0)<\delta_0$, choose distinct primes for $k_0-1$ residual factors in proportional intervals near $X^{1/(2k_0)}$, and choose the last to make $sV$ near $\sqrt X/4$ in a fixed proportional interval. The last prime has exponent $1/(2k_0)-o(1)$, so every residual prime lies strictly between $e^{4T}$ and the old split. The prime number theorem supplies these choices with a different largest prime near $2\sqrt X$ and a similarly positioned right operand using disjoint primes. The two actual operands can be put strictly inside $\mathcal J$ and have an even sum, both zero floors and a nonempty ordinary long/product row. They have genuine composite cofactors but no upper prime at this test split.

## 2. Compute the actual rejected-root term at the double nonlong floors

Fix $a=b=41/2$. In the exact double-nonlong row the only allowed short containing labels are both one. Indeed $Fz\le D_N$ and the true lower prime floor imply $z\le e^{T_N/2}$, below every cofactor prime for large $X$; the same holds on the right. The arithmetic marks are therefore $\log h,\log k$, with the external **minus** from (5) of the capped theorem. Since this row's intrinsic bits are $(0,0)$, the true Walsh choice $(1,1)$ multiplies these marks by $\mu(h)\mu(k)$. After restoring actual prime-floor and integer-cut shifts at the end, the common prime-root interval on each arm is

$$
        20\le u=\frac{L/2-\log F}{T}\le41/2,\qquad hF\in\mathcal J. \tag{4}
$$

The literal lower floor and $F\le D_N$ differ from these fixed two threshold logs by $O(1)$ on the target window, using the same monotone log comparison and integer floor. The true positive enclosing prime slivers cost $O(X^{3/2}\ell/L)$ in target norm, negligible in (3), with all other hard conditions retained in that comparison. Temporary target-nonunit deletion is also negligible: any offending prime factor is at least $e^{5T/4}$, divides $N$, and the positive overlap count is bounded by the old reciprocal multiple estimate with at most two rooted choices.

On the rooted unbinned arm of (4) the full-signed fourth norm is $O_B(X^{3/4}\ell^{-B})$ by Section 1. For a rejected root with $F<P$, the unique other near-square-root prime is $P$ in the cofactor. Write $h=d_0P$, $d_0=sW$, with all factors distinct on this cofactor and $\log d_0\le42T$. The restriction follows from $P>F$, (4) and the physical window. It leaves no other competing large factor. On this rejected part the genuine sign is $\mu(h)=\mu(W)$: $s$ and $P$ are distinct primes and their two signs cancel. A rejected repeat $F\mid h$ has physical integer $d_0F^2$, again with $d_0\le e^{42T}$; this component is disjoint from $F<P$ for large $X$, since three near-square-root factors counting multiplicities cannot occur. Its support has size at most $X^{1/2+o(1)}$, and weight per integer is at most a fixed power of $L$. Interpolating the coefficient $\ell^1$ and Fourier $L^2$ bounds gives fourth norm at most $X^{3/8+o(1)}$, negligible. Thus repeats and exact equality at the integer cut never enter the real collision coefficient.

Let $\mathcal B$ be the actual Fourier polynomial of the strict rejected-root terms $F<P$ with (4) and mark $\mu(W)\log h$. On the old auxiliary majors apply Siegel–Walfisz in $F$, at fixed $h$; its interval includes the literal strict upper endpoint $P$. Since all cofactor primes exceed the polylogarithmic denominator, the exact rational numerator factor is $\mu(q)/\varphi(q)$. With $x=hF$ the real weight is $\log h/(h\log F)$. At fixed $x,d_0$ replace the remaining prime $P$ in its actual interval by the ordinary reciprocal real measure. This uses no congruence in $P$, because the numerator factor has already been evaluated. Both prime replacements have arbitrary inverse-logarithmic precision on the original auxiliary arcs, uniformly in the fixed sharp endpoints, as in the earlier hard-prime comparison.

The change of variable in (4) has $\log P=\log x-\log d_0-L/2+uT$ and $d\log P=T\,du$. On the compact range of contributing $d_0$, $T/\log P=(2/\ell)(1+O(\ell^{-1}))$ and $\log h/\log F=1+O(\ell^{-1})$. The strict comparison $P>F$ is precisely

$$
             2u>\log(sW)/T-(\log x-L)/T.                            \tag{5}
$$

Consequently the real density on $\mathcal J$, to supremum and total-variation error $O(\ell^{-2})$, is the constant

$$
 \frac2\ell\sum_{s\ {\rm old}}\frac1s
       \sum_W\frac{\mu(W)}W
       \int_{20}^{41/2}\mathbf1_{\,2u>\log(sW)/T}\,du .               \tag{6}
$$

Here the $W$ primes are precisely the allowed cofactor primes on the fixed log range; one may impose $\log W\le42T$ before the prefix constraint. To check the variation assertion directly, at fixed $s,W$ the true $x$-dependent lower endpoint in (5) moves by at most $O(|x-y|/(XT))$. On common regions the ratios of the original logarithms have variation $O(|x-y|/(XL))$. After the $\ell^{-1}$ scale, both errors are smaller than $\ell^{-2}$; the endpoint jumps at the fixed physical interval cost only the supremum error. The positive lower reciprocal mass on this bounded log-ratio band is $O(1)$. This proves the assertion for the real geometric density. The separate discrete PNT errors are integrated in supremum and do not need a derivative.

For $t=\log s/T$ in its fixed range, the signed reciprocal prefix of $W$ in (6) is, uniformly in $u,t$,

$$
 \sum_{\log W/T\le2u-t}\frac{\mu(W)}W
    =\rho\!\left(\frac{2u-t}{4}\right)+O_B(L^{-B})                  \tag{7}
$$

for every fixed $B$. To see this, expand ordered distinct prime tuples on the bounded log range from $4$ to $42$, with their signs and factorial denominators. Replace the one-prime reciprocal measures by $dv/v$ at fixed other coordinates by hard-interval reciprocal PNT, and delete repetitions by the square reciprocal tail. Only a uniformly bounded number of factors can fit under the prefix bound. The resulting signed convolution exponential, including its atom at zero, is the Dickman cumulative rescaled by $4$, exactly as in the full-sign calculation. The same result holds at strict or weak prefix bounds, since the reciprocal error has the appropriate discrete endpoint and the continuous prefix has no atom in this range. Reciprocal PNT in the old primes then makes (6) precisely $\mathfrak c/\ell+O_B(L^{-B})$, with $\mathfrak c$ in (2). The function $\rho$ is strictly positive at every finite nonnegative argument by its delay identity, so the constant is positive, however small.

We also need the genuine minor estimate for this collision polynomial, not only a real integral. Keep $hF\in\mathcal J$ and the extra strict inequality $F<P(h)$ as separate integer Perron cutoffs, with $P(h)$ uniquely determined on its cofactor support. For the latter use the half-integer boundary $P(h)-1/2$. The Perron integrals twist the cofactor coefficients by bounded unit-modulus factors of $h$ and $P(h)-1/2$, and twist the prime coefficients by powers of $F$, so the same bilinear minor bound and truncation estimates as for the two hard cuts in the original parity proof apply. The positive separation at half-integers permits a fixed sufficiently large polynomial truncation height, with only polylogarithmic integrated overhead. Both cofactor and prime dyads are in the same old near-square-root windows and the cofactor coefficient bounds are unchanged. Thus the collision minor fourth norms are arbitrary inverse-logarithmic. This avoids treating a moving strict prime comparison as an unproved Fourier smoothing error.

It follows that the major transform of $\mathcal B$ at $a/q+\beta$ is $(\mu(q)/\varphi(q))$ times the window transform with constant density $\mathfrak c/\ell$, plus a real-transform error $O(\ell^{-2}\min(X,|\beta|^{-1}))$ times the same rational factor, and arbitrary inverse-logarithmic discrete errors. The canonical signed arm is rooted minus the strict rejected part minus repeats. Thus its two-arm square differs from $\mathcal B^2$ by $O(X^{3/2}\ell^{-3})$ after taking target coefficients in $\ell^2$, by Hölder and Parseval and the stated strong smallness of the other two terms. In the collision square, sum reduced numerators **before** absolute values as in the [exact rational completion](r80-source-local-mean-square-at-sign-transition.md), §3. The moment $\|c_q(N)\|_2/\varphi(q)^2\ll_{\varepsilon}\sqrt X\,q^{-3/2+\varepsilon}$ is summable over the squarefree denominators. The real cross-error is $O(\ell^{-3}\min(X,|\beta|^{-1})^2)$, so its target norm is $O(X^{3/2}\ell^{-3})$; extending the finite beta ranges and the denominator sum likewise costs less by choosing the original fixed log power large enough. The exact physical convolution of the two fixed indicators is $B_{\mathcal J}(N)$. With the true external double-nonlong minus, and restoration of the actual floor and unit conditions, this proves (3).

Finally $\|\Sigma(N)\|_2\ll\sqrt X$ and $B_{\mathcal J}(N)\ll X$ give the upper scale, while on a fixed interior window the even singular factor has an absolute positive lower bound and $B_{\mathcal J}(N)\gg X$. There are $\gg X$ even targets on such a window. These facts and (3) give the matching lower squared norm and the stated exceptional count by Chebyshev at the genuine scale $X/\ell^2$. The comparison attributes this leading signed term to the actual canonical root veto, rather than to a loss in an unsigned error estimate.
