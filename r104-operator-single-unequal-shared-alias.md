# One unequal shared relation forces conductor on the actual alias

A single unequal mixed power already detects much of the shared-prime part of the original alias. Its conductor outside the denominator factor is forced locally by the difference of its exponents. This gives exact support exclusions and a count of possible denominators for two earlier small-conductor selectors. It gives no operator norm estimate.

## 1. Exact local law for one unequal relation

Keep the genuine alias $q=2k$, reduced rational numerator modulo $b$, progression modulus $q'=qb$, and true shared factorization of [R103 §1](r103-operator-independent-shared-relation-obstruction.md#1-live-coefficients-and-the-determinant-restriction):

$$
b=dm,\quad d=b_\parallel,\quad m=b_\perp,\qquad
 q=r_bh_q,\qquad s=dh_q,\qquad q'=r_bms .
 \tag{1}
$$

The shared primes are precisely the primes of $d$ and of $h_q$. At a live original character-pair coefficient the shared leg characters $\gamma,\gamma'$ are primitive on $s$, and their product is the lift of the actual full-product component $\nu_d\bmod d$. Here “live” means nonzero at an original primitive-numerator orientation before detuning, not nonzero after summing over different coefficients. Both facts are exact: they are the unit-fiber primitivity of [R103 §2](r103-operator-independent-shared-relation-obstruction.md#2-proof-using-the-literal-unit-fibers) and the retained-product identity of [R100 §2](r100-operator-three-factor-joint-powers.md#2-exact-joint-fibers-at-one-full-product).

For arbitrary distinct integers $a,c$, put $w=a-c\ne0$, $P=\gamma^a(\gamma')^c$, $C_P=\operatorname{cond}(P)$, using the primitive inducing conductor. At $p\mid d$ write

$$
e_p=v_p(d)\ge1,\quad \ell_p=v_p(h_q)\ge1,\quad
        A_p=e_p+\ell_p,\quad v=v_p(|w|).
$$

Define $\bar\ell_p=\ell_p$, except that $\bar\ell_2=\ell_2-1$ if $e_2=1,\ \ell_2\ge2$. Thus $\bar\ell_p\ge1$ at all shared primes. Put $\bar h=\prod_{p\mid d}p^{\bar\ell_p}$; this is $h_q$ or $h_q/2$, according to the indicated case. Then the $p$-part of the actual conductor satisfies

$$
\begin{cases}
       v_p(C_P)=A_p-v,&v<\bar\ell_p,\\
       v_p(C_P)\le e_p,&v\ge\bar\ell_p .
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2}
$$

In the exceptional case $p=2,\ e_2=1,\ \ell_2\ge2$ the exponent in the second line is exactly zero. In particular

$$
C_P\mid d \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \bar h\mid w .
 \tag{3}
$$

No assertion of cancellation is made on primes where the second line allows different local conductors.

To prove this, the forced partner expresses the same character as

$$
P=\gamma^{w}(\nu_d\circ\pi_s)^c ,
 \tag{4}
$$

where $\pi_s$ is the unit reduction from $s$ to $d$. Thus the second factor has conductor dividing $d$, also for negative $c$. If a local first factor has conductor exponent strictly greater than $e_p$, multiplying by the second factor cannot change that exponent: the latter is trivial on the last kernel subgroup on which the former is nontrivial. If both exponents are at most $e_p$, the product is also induced from $p^{e_p}$.

At an odd prime, a primitive character of modulus $p^{A_p}$, $A_p\ge2$, has wild order exactly $p^{A_p-1}$. If $v<\ell_p$, then $A_p-v>e_p\ge1$, so the wild part survives with order $p^{A_p-1-v}$. Its new conductor exponent is $A_p-v$. If $v\ge\ell_p$, the surviving wild conductor exponent is at most $A_p-v\le e_p$; if the wild part disappears, the tame part has exponent at most one. This proves both lines at odd primes.

At $2^{A_2}$ with $A_2\ge3$, the primitive order on the cyclic subgroup generated by $5$ is $2^{A_2-2}$. When $e_2\ge2$ and $v<\ell_2$, $A_2-v\ge3$, and the new wild conductor exponent is $A_2-v$, exceeding $e_2$. For $v\ge\ell_2$ the wild conductor exponent is at most $e_2$, and the sign part could only have exponent two. When $A_2=2$ one has $e_2=\ell_2=1$, and the primitive sign character retains conductor $4$ for odd $w$ and becomes trivial for even $w$. Finally if $e_2=1,\ \ell_2\ge2$, the lifted $\nu_d$ is trivial on the $2$-part. For $v<\ell_2-1$ the wild part survives at exponent $A_2-v\ge3$; for $v\ge\ell_2-1\ge1$ both the wild and sign parts are killed. This proves the adjusted exponent and (2) at every prime. The case $s=1$ uses the empty product and no restrictions.

## 2. Consequences for a small conductor ceiling

For a nonzero $w$, write $d_{\mid w}$ for the full part of $d$ supported on primes dividing $w$, and $d_{\nmid w}=d/d_{\mid w}$; likewise define $s_{\nmid w}$. The divisibilities from (2) include

$$
\begin{split}
  s_{\nmid w}&\mid C_P,\\
  d_{\nmid w}\,\frac{\bar h}{\gcd(\bar h,|w|)}&\mid C_P,\\
  \left(\frac{\operatorname{rad}(d)}
             {\operatorname{rad}(\gcd(d,|w|))}\right)^2&\mid C_P .
 \end{split}
 \tag{5}
$$

Indeed, for $p\nmid w$ the first line of (2) gives the full exponent $A_p$, and each such prime has $A_p\ge2$. This proves the first and third divisibilities, and also bounds the local exponent $e_p+\bar\ell_p$ in the second. For $p\mid w$ the second line of (5) only asks for $p^{\bar\ell_p-v}$ when that exponent is positive. This follows from the first line of (2). In particular $C_P=s$ when $(w,s)=1$. Also

$$
\operatorname{rad}(d)\le |w|\sqrt{C_P},\qquad
    h_q\le2|w|\, C_P,\qquad
    d_{\mid w}\ge d/C_P .
 \tag{6}
$$

The factor two comes only from $h_q/\bar h\le2$. For the last inequality use $d_{\nmid w}\mid s_{\nmid w}\mid C_P$.

Now allow any shared exponent pairs in $|a|,|c|\le V$, $a\ne c$, with $1\le V\le (\log Q)^{C_0}$ for fixed $C_0$. Impose $d\ge D_0$ and $C_P\le L$ for at least one such pair, with $D_0,L\ge1$. There is no live coefficient for this selector on a given actual alias and denominator if

$$
\operatorname{rad}(d)>2V\sqrt L,\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 h_q>4V L .
 \tag{7}
$$

There is also no live coefficient if the part of $s$ supported on primes $p>2V$ exceeds $L$, by $s_{\nmid w}\mid C_P$. These are exact support exclusions, not bounds on coefficients after summing signs.

For denominators $b\le B$ in the polynomial parameter ranges, the number that can support any live coefficient with these gates, even allowing all actual aliases and all exponent pairs in the box, is bounded by both $B$ and

$$
O_{\epsilon,C_0}\!\left(
                         Q^\epsilon B L/D_0\right).
 \tag{8}
$$

If $D_0>B$ the count is zero. Otherwise (6) forces $d_{\mid w}\ge D_0/L$; this integer divides $b$ and is supported entirely on primes dividing $w$. For each fixed $1\le|w|\le2V$, there are $O_\epsilon(Q^\epsilon)$ candidates $t\le B$ with that prime support restriction, without regard to the alias. For completeness, for fixed $\delta>0$ the number is at most

$$
B^\delta\prod_{p\mid w}(1-p^{-\delta})^{-1}
                       \le B^\delta |w|^{O_\delta(1)}
$$

by a positive Rankin majorant; this includes $|w|=1$, where only $t=1$ occurs. Choose $\delta$ sufficiently small in the fixed polynomial range of $B$. Each $t\ge D_0/L$ divides at most $B/t\le BL/D_0$ denominators. Summing over these candidates, and over the polylogarithmic number of differences $w$, proves (8) by using smaller intermediate epsilons. This is a count of distinct denominators, with no assertion about the sizes or cancellation of the live coefficients on those denominators.

## 3. Application to the actual old unequal cuts

The [R99 selector](r99-operator-unequal-shared-leg-powers.md#1-exact-selection-and-packet-estimate) tests $\operatorname{cond}(\gamma^a(\gamma')^c)\le Q^{3/1000}$ at $d\ge Q^{1/5}$, with positive unequal powers from its fixed log-power range. Its reduction was proved on a packet, but (7) and (8) here concern exactly where those discarded pairs can have a nonzero original incidence. In particular they require

$$
\operatorname{rad}(d)\le 2V Q^{3/2000},
                  \qquad h_q\le4V Q^{3/1000}.
$$

At $B=\lfloor Q^{39/100}\rfloor$ there are at most $O_\epsilon(Q^{193/1000+\epsilon})$ possible denominators for this R99 sector.

For the [R100 selector](r100-operator-three-factor-joint-powers.md#1-a-three-factor-sector-of-the-retained-field), take only the part whose actual $(j,u,v)$ triples lie in the earlier [R96 selection range](r96-operator-equal-and-signed-joint-powers.md#1-exact-enlargement-and-packet-bound). Here $j$ is the alias exponent, and $u,v$ the two exponents on the coprime denominator factor; the shared exponents of R100 remain unequal as stated there. The R96 gate then forces
$\operatorname{cond}(\alpha^j)\operatorname{cond}(\eta^u(\eta')^v)>D_J$
for each proposed triple on a retained pair. A R100 joint conductor ceiling $D$ consequently entails the genuine shared ceiling $L=D/D_J$, and if $D\le D_J$ the tested set is empty. This use of R96 is only on the covered triples; it is not available simply from high individual conductors. The actual example $D=Q^{7/10}$, $D_J=Q^{3/5}$, $D_0=Q^{1/5}$ thus gives $C_P\le Q^{1/10}$ in the covered part. It requires

$$
\operatorname{rad}(d)\le 2V Q^{1/20},
                       \qquad h_q\le4V Q^{1/10}.
$$

In the same high band, at most $O_\epsilon(Q^{29/100+\epsilon})$ denominators can support live coefficients in this covered sector. At the concrete scales of both tests the entire squarefree region $d\ge Q^{1/5}$ is therefore absent from their live selected sets for large $Q$. More generally, the forced $w$-supported part on such sets has size at least $D_0/L$, a power of $Q$ even though $w$ is only polylogarithmic.

The deductions use only the exact original incidence and the named arithmetic selection masks, so do not require Dirichlet GRH. They neither remove more terms of the true field nor bound the norm of any retained or discarded packet. No statement is made about the entire R100 sector if its triples are outside the earlier ranges, about equal shared exponents, or about covariance or weak S.

## 4. Checks of nonvacuity and the exceptional factor at two

The concrete R100 witness of [R100 §4](r100-operator-three-factor-joint-powers.md#4-an-eligible-nonzero-coefficient-after-all-earlier-cuts) already gives a live old joint-selected coefficient under the common exponent ranges. It has $d=2^e$, $h_q=2$, and shared positive powers $(a,c)=(3,1)$. Thus $w=2$, $\bar h=2$, and the shared product of unequal powers is induced from $d$, of conductor $2^{t+2}\asymp Q^{1/25}$. Equations (3), (5) and (6) permit this drop: its original shared leg is still primitive. In contrast, an odd $w$ at that same shared modulus would force full conductor $2^{e+1}$, regardless of the precise primitive leg.

The factor at $2$ in (3) cannot in general be omitted, even at an actual primitive-numerator incidence. Take $q=4,b=2,d=2,h_q=4,s=q'=8$ and the same primitive character $\gamma=\gamma'$ on both legs, defined by $\gamma(-1)=1,\gamma(5)=-1$. Its product is principal on the shared denominator, so the genuine pairing holds. For a fixed odd $x\bmod8$ in any original orientation $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$, the two eligible $y$'s are $y_0=-\sigma_1\sigma_2 x\bmod8$ and $y_0+4$. At numerator $1\bmod2$, the character product and exponential are both one for the first choice. In the second, each factor changes sign: multiplication by $1+4$ on units modulo $8$ is the shift by $4$, and the phase changes by $e(-\sigma_2/2)=-1$. Thus the original local sum is nonzero, of magnitude $8$. For $(a,c)=(3,1)$ the unequal power is principal, although $h_q=4$ does not divide $w=2$. Here $\bar h=2$ and $h_q/\gcd(h_q,|w|)=2$, so the factor two in the general inequality in (6) is real. This last example verifies the exact local boundary, not a witness inside the large-$d$ numerical gates.
