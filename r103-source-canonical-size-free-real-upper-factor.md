# The actual canonical largest-prime carrier without a lower size cap

Keep the [complete-alphabet canonical carrier](r102-source-canonical-full-alphabets-size-gate.md), all its genuine seven signed rows and Walsh convention, old prime $s$, minimum remaining prime $e^{4T}$, fixed prime-floor box $0\le a,b\le A_0$, and true split $Z_0\le Z\le X^{\delta_0}$. Write $L=\log X$, $\ell=\log L$, $T=L/\ell$, $\theta=1/20$, $Z_0=\exp(2T\ell^\theta)$. Now remove the remaining lower cap $W\le X^{\epsilon/2}$, but **keep $U>1$** on each arm $h=sWU$ or $k=s'W'U'$. Equivalently the cofactor still contains a prime strictly above the true split. The distinguished prime is the actual largest prime of the physical integer, and the squarefree $N$-unit conditions, hard physical intervals, real external prime floors and original literal long/product tests remain in force. The resulting signed Walsh coefficients are denoted $W^{\mathrm{sf},\mathbf u}_j$, where the superscript uses this size-free lower part, not any changed squarefree convention.

For $\tau=\log Z/L$ and an auxiliary real lower endpoint $\xi<\tau$, write $D^\pm_{\xi,\tau}(v)$ for the density of

$$
 \exp_*\!\left(\pm\mathbf1_{\xi<u\le .7}\frac{du}{u}\right)
       -\exp_*\!\left(\pm\mathbf1_{\xi<u\le\tau}\frac{du}{u}\right).
                                                                    \tag{1}
$$

The measure is the real reciprocal product with at least one prime above the split, with full factor sign if the minus is used. Both atoms at zero cancel. All occurrences below use its continuous representative on the compact range of the model. Let

$$
 \mathcal C^{\mathrm{sf}}_{X,Z}
   =\frac{|\mathcal J|}{\ell}
       \sum_{s\ {\rm old}}\frac{D^+_{4/\ell,\tau}(1/2-\log s/L)}s ,
 \qquad
 \mathcal N^{\mathrm{sf}}_{N,Z}
   =\Sigma(N) B_{\mathcal J}(N)
                     (\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sf}}_{X,Z}/|\mathcal J|)^2. \tag{2}
$$

Keep the same seven continuum phases $A_j(a,b)$ and intrinsic bits $\mathbf b_j$. Put $m=m_j(\mathbf u)$ for the number of surviving full-cofactor sign factors in the actual Walsh coefficient.

**Theorem.** Uniformly $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sf}}_{X,Z}\asymp X$. Put $w_0=w_1=1$ and $w_2=2$. At fixed common $a,b,Z$, the squared target norm of the maximum over the original rows and bits with the indicated $m$, after subtracting exactly $\mathbf1_{\mathbf u=\mathbf b_j}\mathcal N^{\mathrm{sf}}_{N,Z}A_j(a,b)$, is $O(X^3\ell^{-2w_m})$. With $r\in\{0,1,2\}$ free floor axes and fully target-adaptive true split and row/bit, the squared norm of the common maximum of the same error is $O(X^3\ell^{-2w_m/(r+2)})$. Constants may depend on the fixed carrier and floor box. This estimates the specified largest-prime rows with a real upper prime, not unrestricted divisor sums, a prescribed exceptional target, or Hypothesis S.

## 1. The real at-least-one condition and signed cancellation

The old exact row algebra is unchanged. In particular $h=sWU$ is composite because $U>1$; the containing complete sum vanishes by $\Lambda(h)=0$. The same divisor/floor arguments keep all active cofactor logs at $L/2+O_{A_0}(T)$ and all active short labels below $M=\exp((24+2A_0)T)<Z_0$. In the analytic comparison temporarily admit every near-square-root candidate prime root. The squarefree cofactor, old-prime choices, and actual upper-prime test stay in the rooted polynomial. The largest and prime-repeat vetoes will be restored arm by arm. Candidate root multiplicity is at most two and the short-label multiplicity is still $\ell^{O(1)}$: every short label only admits a fixed number of primes $\ge e^{4T}$ even if the actual lower factor has $O(\ell)$ primes.

For clarity, view all prime factors of the cofactor after $s$ as a single rough product rather than conditioning on a potentially very large lower factor. On the range $[\xi,.7]$, with $\xi=4/\ell$ or a fixed constant multiple used below, replace the discrete weighted reciprocal prime measure by $du/u$. The hard-cut replacement of the [growing-residual proof](r75-source-dynamic-giant-growing-rough-residual.md), §2, applies equally to these complete, contiguous intervals: in ordered tuples expand by arity with factorial denominators, replace one prime at a time with the other coordinates fixed, and delete repetitions by the reciprocal prime-square tail. The total reciprocal mass is $O(\log\ell)$ and the prime scale is at least $e^{4T}$, so errors against each fixed hard interval or bounded-variation mark are arbitrary inverse logarithmic even after the summed arities. The signs are exactly $(-1)^k$ at arity $k$. Subtract the completely truncated alphabet up to $Z$; this is the exact condition of a genuine upper prime, with strict and weak endpoints used in their literal discrete sums. The cap $.7$ excludes no prime from an active cofactor. The resulting real measure is (1). This argument also applies with fixed positive occupation weights at most two over a fixed log-ratio band, needed in the positive all-label envelopes.

We record useful density estimates explicitly. Write $B_{\xi,b}=\exp_*(\mathbf1_{\xi<u\le b}\,du/u)$, with the atom at zero understood. Away from zero the density of this positive measure is $O(1/\xi)$ uniformly in $v$: multiply the density by its argument $v$ and mark one coordinate, then restrict the remaining measures to shifts at most $v$. Their mass is at most $\exp(\int_\xi^{\max(v,\xi)}du/u)=\max(1,v/\xi)$, and the density is zero for $v<\xi$. This proves the asserted bound for $v\ge\xi$ as well. On a fixed compact positive interval below $.7$ and above both lower-scale hard edges, the density is Lipschitz with the same order bound whenever the upper endpoint is $.7$ or bounded strictly away below the compact. This follows by differentiating the marked interval convolution: its boundary translates either vanish or meet ordinary bounded densities, not their atoms. Thus $D^+_{\xi,\tau}$ is nonnegative, globally $O(1/\xi)$ away from its canceled atom, and has compact Lipschitz bound $O(1/\xi)$ where used here. Also $|D^-_{\xi,\tau}|\le D^+_{\xi,\tau}$ almost everywhere by the original at-least-one tuple expansion. Each pointwise bound used under a real integral therefore holds independently of density endpoint convention.

For the signed bound let $A_\xi=\exp_*(-\mathbf1_{u>\xi}\,du/u)$ on the nonnegative real line. On compact ranges it is the signed Dickman measure of [the full-sign calculation](r98-source-tag-full-sign-stretched-cancellation.md), §3. More explicitly it is the unit atom plus density $-\rho(v/\xi-1)/v$. The cumulative is $\rho(v/\xi)$, decreases to zero, and satisfies factorial decay by $x\rho(x)=\int_{x-1}^x\rho(u)\,du$. Consequently

$$
      A_\xi(\mathbb R)=0,\qquad
      \|A_\xi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le2,\qquad
      \int s\,d|A_\xi|(s)\ll \xi.                                  \tag{3}
$$

For the moment, cancel $s$ in its density and substitute $u=s/\xi-1$; the resulting $\xi\int_0^\infty\rho(u)du$ is finite by the same decay. Let $B_\tau=B_{\tau,.7}=\delta_0+b_\tau(v)\,dv$. The convolution exponentials give the exact identity, on the real support below $.7$,

$$
 \exp_*(-\mathbf1_{\xi<u\le\tau}\,du/u)=A_\xi*B_\tau,\qquad
             D^-_{\xi,\tau}(v)=-(A_\xi*b_\tau)(v).                    \tag{4}
$$

In the second identity the atom of $B_\tau$ cancels the whole $A_\xi$ from subtraction in (1), not just its atom. No behavior beyond the compact range is needed for (4); the global extension of $A_\xi$ is used only for (3).

The preceding positive marking bound gives $\|b_\tau\|_\infty\ll \tau^{-1}$, extending by zero on negative arguments. On $[.2,.6]$ it also gives $\operatorname{Lip}(b_\tau)\ll\tau^{-1}$, since the inherited fixed split cap $\delta_0$ is small and the other edge is $.7$. For $v\in[.4,.6]$, subtract $b_\tau(v)$ in the integral against the whole signed measure, using its zero mass. At shifts $s\le v/2$ the difference is $O(s/\tau)$. At larger shifts it is $O(1/\tau)$ and their absolute mass is $O(\xi)$ by (3). Hence

$$
                         |D^-_{\xi,\tau}(v)|\ll \xi/\tau
                             \qquad(.4\le v\le.6).                 \tag{5}
$$

This is a signed estimate for the real cofactor with an actual upper prime. It would be incorrect to simply estimate the two terms of (1) by their unsigned masses.

## 2. The actual pair bounds and calibration

For the labeled short arms, first fix the old prime $s$ and the squarefree product of all other primes in the fixed short band $[e^{4T},e^{A T}]$, with $A>24+2A_0$ fixed. Call that product $D_{\mathrm l}$. These primes are below $Z_0$, so they determine all short labels and do not supply the required upper prime. Use $\xi=A/\ell$ in the remaining exact reciprocal replacement (1). The positive labeled reciprocal mass of the fixed short block is $O(1)$ by its fixed prime log ratio, allowing occupation factor at most two for eligible primes and the two old-prime choices. In this positive product measure $\mathbb E\log D_{\mathrm l}=O(T)$. Also the positive moment of $\exp(\log D_{\mathrm l}/(AT))$ is bounded relative to the same mass, by its Euler product since $p^{1/(AT)}\le e$ throughout the fixed band. Thus the mass where $\log D_{\mathrm l}>.05L$ is exponentially small in $\ell$, enough to be smaller than any fixed negative power of $\ell$. Off the compact range use the global positive density bound $O(\xi^{-1})$; the at-least-one measure has no atom. This controls that discarded tail also when a shortened cofactor interval meets the support edge.

Use normalized hard bins of width $\delta=\ell^{-3}$ in the two [actual label coordinates](r89-source-four-actual-parity-phases.md). At fixed physical $x=hF$ and fixed short label the log cofactor interval has width at most $\delta/\ell$ in the $\log h/L$ variable. For the small low-block shifts the remaining log argument belongs to $[.4,.6]$; here use (5) with the bounded literal prime marks and their scaled physical derivative marks on common intervals. After the old auxiliary major-arc transform and fourth-mean argument, the rooted full-sign bin has fourth norm

$$
 O(X^{3/4}\delta\,\ell^{-2}/\tau)
                    =O(X^{3/4}\delta\,\ell^{-1-\theta}),            \tag{6}
$$

since $\tau\ge 2\ell^{\theta-1}$. The discarded low-block tail is smaller. Unrestricted positive bin fourth norm is $O(X^{3/4}\delta)$. In the physical variation calculation the symmetric difference of clipped intervals is handled positively; its relative scale is $O(\ell/L)$, negligible at (6), and on the common part only the bare real mark is differentiated. Siegel–Walfisz in the prime at fixed cofactor and the half-integer Perron separations of the original hard products apply unchanged. There is no new inverse-bin-width loss; discrete replacement and auxiliary minor errors remain arbitrary inverse-logarithmic by the rooted multiplicity and label coefficient bound.

Restore the true largest and squarefree support on each accepted arm. The collision argument of the complete-alphabet canonical theorem uses no lower cap: a removed root has a prime $P\ge F$ in its cofactor (including a repeat), and then $P>Z$ while the remaining product is at most $e^{C T}<Z_0$. Thus that arm has exactly one upper prime and all other prime factors lie in a fixed log-ratio band. The rejected signed arm is dominated by the same positive one-prime envelope as before, with fourth norm $O(X^{3/4}\delta/\ell)$ on a bin, or $O(X^{3/4}/\ell)$ unbinned. The envelope calculation is independent of the removed size cap: lower reciprocal mass in that log band is bounded and the upper single-prime density is bounded on its compact range. In particular (6) and this exact subtraction yield true signed bin fourth norm $O(X^{3/4}\delta/\ell)$.

For each signed row the bin pairs accepted by all actual tests separate before taking the pair product, after the usual temporary unit/floor comparisons. Hölder and Parseval on the two canonical bin arms thus cost $O(X^{3/2}\delta^2\ell^{-m})$ per accepted pair if $m=1,2$. There are $O(\delta^{-2})$ such bins. The $O(\delta^{-1})$ ambiguous bins keep their genuine integer tests; they cost $O(X^{3/2}\delta)$ positively and do not limit either rate. Here the normalized moving boundaries have displacement $O(1/T)=o(\delta)$. The standard positive floor and unit restoration bounds remain negligible, with the same smallest factor and the bounded number of possible roots. This proves the two zero-comparator fixed estimates on the original arithmetic coefficients.

In the matching channel, use the same real short-label scalar comparison as in the complete-alphabet theorem. On a bin the exact scalar uses $D^+_{\xi,\tau}$ and the actual fixed short block, with bounded main density $O(\delta)$ and transform error of density scale $O(\delta/\ell+1/T)$; the $1/T$ term is the ordinary translated endpoint, and the compact Lipschitz estimate just proved supplies the same density variation as before. The reduced-numerator rational completion is unchanged. Its summed target cost is $O(X^{3/2}\ell^{-1})$, since $(T\delta)^{-1}=o(\ell^{-1})$. The seven signed areas are still the separate bounded Lipschitz kernels of the [seven-row proof](r95-source-off-axis-right-forbidden-dominance.md), including both mixed orientations and the two double pieces. In each, remove the short-block log argument from the remaining density. Its mean log size $O(T)$ costs $O(\ell^{-1})$ at the normalized scalar scale, using the compact Lipschitz bound and the exponentially small far-block tail. There is no size strip or arity loss here. The signed short Euler law then gives exactly the old short measure in the two area variables, since the short prime choices, old prime, signs and cutoff below $Z_0$ have not changed.

To identify the detached *positive* factor with (2), convolve the positive remaining at-least-one measure with $B_{4/\ell,\xi}$. This is exactly (1) with lower endpoint $4/\ell$, because all the added prime coordinates are below $\tau$. The small positive continuous measure has total mass $A/4$ and first displacement moment $O(\ell^{-1})$; its large shifts beyond a fixed positive constant are exponentially rare by the same exponential moment. The discrete positive lower block product also has mass $A/4+O_B(L^{-B})$ for every fixed $B$, by reciprocal PNT on that fixed log-ratio band and the reciprocal square error. This compares the detached factor directly to (2) with error $O(\ell^{-1})$ after the factor $1/\ell$. The old prime measure and reciprocal normalization are unchanged. Thus the signed real area scalar for each row is $(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sf}}_{X,Z}/|\mathcal J|)^2 A_j(a,b)+O(\ell^{-1})$. Multiply by $\Sigma(N)B_{\mathcal J}(N)$ and use $\|\Sigma\|_2\ll\sqrt X$. Restoring canonical roots in the matching case costs $O(X^{3/2}/\ell)$ by the unbinned positive correction. The claimed matching estimate follows.

The upper bound for (2) is immediate from the positive density bound. For the lower, calibrate on the same fixed positive-length reference prime-exponent interval in $(L/2-\log F)/T$ as in the complete-alphabet theorem, by the exact rooted first moment with weight $\log F$ and the hard physical window. The positive reciprocal replacement with lower endpoint $4/\ell$, and the compact Lipschitz bound as the reference exponent and physical coordinate vary, evaluate this moment as the reference length times $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{sf}}_{X,Z}+O(X/\ell)$. This exact rooted reference count contains all rooted size-gated terms, since only a size cap was removed and an actual upper prime still occurs. The old calibration of its size-gated counterpart is the same reference length times its old positive normalization up to $O(X/\ell)$; that normalization is $\gg X$ uniformly. This proves the new lower bound without a pointwise comparison of two different real model densities.

## 3. Split gaps now require failure of the actual upper-factor condition

For a real split gap with lower exponent $u$ and upper exponent $v\le\delta_0$, a lost canonical cofactor has all its prime factors at most $X^v$, with at least one prime in the literal interval $(X^u,X^v]$. This follows from the sole remaining condition $P^+(h)>Z$ on split changes; the canonical root and all marks of a retained pair are intrinsic. The size and arity failure alternatives of the earlier carrier are no longer used.

In a positive all-label comparison allow occupancy weight two for eligible label primes (and both old-prime choices), while marking one occurrence in the literal gap. All marked primes are above $Z_0$, hence outside the short band. In the real marked measure the gap coordinate has weight $dw/w$ and the remaining positive product is wholly truncated at $v$, with normalized minimum $4/\ell$. Its total mass is $O(v\ell)$, since the bounded positive label tilt multiplies the reciprocal product by only a fixed factor. In the marked density argument the cofactor minus the old-prime log is compact, and after the marked shift (at most $v$) it is still bounded away from zero; its remaining density is therefore $O(v\ell)$ by marking one more coordinate in that truncated product. Hence, after inserting the gap prime, the continuous cofactor density is $O(v\ell\log(v/u))$. There is no shifted atom at the evaluation arguments. The same arbitrary inverse-log hard replacement applies with one marked coordinate (the arity factorial argument sums the extra factor at most quadratically in the arity). Integrating over the fixed $O(1/\ell)$ cofactor log window and using the same real-transform variation bound gives fourth norm $O_A(X^{3/4}\{v\log(v/u)+L^{-A}\})$, and the analogous zero-frequency positive bound with factor $X$. In the actual arithmetic expression the indicator of a gap occurrence is bounded by the number of such factors, so this is a positive envelope for the original lost terms, not a claim about their signed monotonicity.

Pair the lost side with the global positive mate, as in the original seven-row proof, keeping both orientations. If $v/u\le2$, then $v\log(v/u)\ll v-u$. Thus each split gap has independent positive target norm $O_A(X^{3/2}(v-u+L^{-A}))$ on the whole true split range, with no separate initial gap. Floor gaps have the old independent enclosing-prime-slab bound $O(X^{3/2}(d+\ell/L))$; the same complete positive density bound proves it here, independently of the other chosen coordinates. For matching comparisons the reference count is nested and loses only the same genuine gap operands, so by calibration the exact normalization change is $O(X(v-u+\ell^{-1}))$. The use of the rooted reference count does not relax the genuine largest condition on the arithmetic rows.

Mesh each free floor axis at maximum spacing $\eta$. On the split axis go geometrically with ratio at most two through the portion below $\eta$ if present, then linearly at spacing at most $\eta$; if $\tau_0=2\ell^{\theta-1}\ge\eta$, use only the linear mesh. Include both endpoints and retain any shortened cells. The number of nodes is $O(\eta^{-1}+\log\ell)=O(\eta^{-1})$ for the stated powers, and the squared sum of widths is $O(\eta)$. Bracket the freely selected tuple below and compare one axis at a time. Since each pointwise positive envelope is independent of the other coordinates, the summed squared error along axes is $O(X^3\eta)$, not multiplied by a product mesh count. In matching the exact comparator difference is $O(X|\Sigma(N)|(\eta+\ell^{-1}))$ pointwise, which is also harmless for its chosen spacing. The $O(\eta^{-(r+1)})$ fixed-node squared budget is $O(X^3\eta^{-(r+1)}\ell^{-2w_m})$. Choosing $\eta=\ell^{-2w_m/(r+2)}$ proves all claimed maxima.

Finally the new size-free support is genuinely larger while the upper-prime requirement remains. At the fixed top split choose an exponent $\epsilon/2<w<1/2-\delta_0$ and a fixed integer $k_0$ such that $w/k_0<\delta_0$. Choose distinct lower primes in fixed proportional windows near $X^{w/k_0}$, an old prime $s$, and an upper prime making $h=sWU$ near $\sqrt X/4$ in a fixed proportional window. This upper prime has exponent $1/2-w-o(1)>\delta_0$. Choose a different largest prime near $2\sqrt X$ and a right mate with disjoint primes so the two physical operands lie inside $\mathcal J$. The ordinary prime number theorem supplies all these choices, and the old geometry gives squarefree units at their even sum, both zero floors, and a nonempty ordinary long/product row. The lower product is $X^{w+o(1)}>X^{\epsilon/2}$, while $U>1$ is strictly satisfied. Nothing in the theorem applies to cofactors without such a real upper factor.
