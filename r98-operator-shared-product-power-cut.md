# Shared-product powers without an alias-conductor condition

A power may lower the conductor of a shared-prime product character, but that loss is bounded by the exponent and one radical factor. Keeping the true conductor in the scalar Gauss estimate, and paying the radical through the literal alias count, removes a sector that can have arbitrarily high alias conductor within the original packet.

## 1. Exact sector and packet estimate

Use the genuine field $Z_{\rm sh}$ retained by [R97](r97-operator-shared-product-joint-conductor.md#1-actual-sector-and-estimate), with its complete even shell, true transported profiles $h,f$, common taper, original $J^{-1}$ normalization and high-denominator packet $\mathcal C_+(B,R)$. Keep original local Dirichlet GRH at the shell targets and auxiliary Dirichlet GRH through $\lceil8MB\rceil+2$, including modulus $1$. Keep all earlier ranges, selections and signs, in particular $k>K\ge A/Q$, $(2k,b)<G$, and $z,w>A$. Both inputs of R97 are allowed, with or without the exact raw R86/R87 parity removals, and with $K\ge2P$ in the former case.

For $q=2k,\ q'=qb$, write

$$
d=b_\parallel=\prod_{p\mid q}p^{v_p(b)},\qquad
 m=b_\perp=b/d,\qquad q_0=qd,\qquad q'=q_0m,\quad(q_0,m)=1 .
 \tag{1}
$$

Write $\nu=\nu_d\rho\bmod b$ for the true full product, with components $\nu_d\bmod d,\rho\bmod m$. Fix $D,D_0\ge1,\ C>0$, and $V_Q=\lfloor(\log Q)^C\rfloor$. Only at aliases with $d\ge D_0$, omit the remaining pairs for which

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\nu_d^u)\le D
                   \quad\text{for some integer }1\le u\le V_Q .
 \tag{2}
$$

All conductors are primitive inducing conductors. There is no test on the alias-conductor factor in this new deletion: R97 had a joint cutoff with that factor, which is still in force beforehand. The full product and all previous component or raw masks are unchanged on the new retained field $Z_{\rm shpow}$. Every selection is made before the original detunings.

With $R_*=1+R$ and the packet norm of R97,

$$
\|Z_{\rm sh}-Z_{\rm shpow}\|_{\rm pkt}
     \ll_{\Gamma,\Theta,C,\epsilon}
         Q^\epsilon \frac{B^{3/2}MD}{D_0}
          \left[\frac{R_*^{3/2}+R_*^{5/2}}Q+
                              \frac{R_*^{1/2}}S\right].
 \tag{3}
$$

The bound permits all prime powers in $b$, and also holds after a fixed orthogonal spatial compression, packet restriction, or deletion only on a subset of the eligible aliases fixed by $k,b$.

At $B=\lfloor Q^{39/100}\rfloor,\ M=Q^{3/5},\ R=\lfloor Q^{1/400}\rfloor,\ S=Q^{249/250}$, take $D_0=Q^{1/5},\ D=Q^{3/1000}$, keeping the previous thresholds as in R97's example. Then (3) is $O_{\epsilon,C}(Q^{-23/4000+\epsilon})$, and the inherited post-parity sharp $\widehat T,\widehat P$ comparison remains $O_{\epsilon,C}(Q^{-1/500+\epsilon})$, now to $Z_{\rm shpow}$. This is a deletion only on the specified packet and gated aliases. It neither estimates the surviving covariance nor gives a statement at a prescribed even modulus, on other packets, or on weak S.

## 2. The actual conductor in the scalar Gauss bound

For every character $\omega\bmod d$ and positive integer $u$, one has

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\omega)
          \le 2u\,\operatorname{rad}(d)\operatorname{cond}(\omega^u).
 \tag{4}
$$

To verify it, fix $p\mid d$, let $p^a$ be the conductor of the local factor, and write $e=v_p(u)$. At odd $p$, when $a\ge2$ its wild order is exactly $p^{a-1}$: the cyclic unit group at the primitive level has this wild part, and the character is nontrivial on its last order-$p$ subgroup. If $e<a-1$ the order after powering is $p^{a-1-e}$, giving conductor exponent $a-e$. Otherwise the entire original $p^a$ is at most $p^{e+1}$, which also bounds the ratio to the new conductor. That ratio bound includes original exponent $a\le1$. At $p=2$ use the sign factor and the cyclic factor generated by $5$, of order $2^{a-2}$ when $a\ge3$. If $e<a-2$, the nontrivial wild part has new conductor exponent $a-e$; otherwise the entire original $2^a$ is at most $2^{e+2}$. The same ratio bound holds for $a\le2$. Multiply these bounds over the primes of $d$. The product of the powers $p^{v_p(u)}$ on those primes divides $u$, and the extra factor at $2$ costs at most $2$ beyond the radical. This proves (4), including trivial moduli and imprimitive factors.

At fixed $q,b,\nu$, the exact incidence forces the character partner $\xi'=(\nu\circ\pi)\bar\xi$, where $\pi$ is unit reduction to $b$. For arbitrary complex unit residue vectors $U,V$, let $\mathscr C_\nu(U,V)$ be the scalar coefficient of $\nu(a)$ in the actual numerator incidence. Retaining the true local conductor in the mixed CRT proof of [R91 §2](r91-operator-denominator-product-power-conductors.md#2-a-mixed-crt-bound-for-the-true-restricted-numerator-vector) gives

$$
|\mathscr C_\nu(U,V)|
          \ll_\epsilon Q^\epsilon
                    \sqrt{\operatorname{cond}(\nu_d)}\,\|U\|_2\|V\|_2 .
 \tag{5}
$$

Here is the exact point where the smaller factor is retained. Under (1), the congruence lives on $q_0=qd$, and the numerator phase there is at denominator $d$ up to multiplication of the reduced numerator by a fixed unit. The original full character pairing on $q_0$ has product lifted from $\nu_d$. The R82 Gauss $L^1$ bound used in R91 bounds this local coefficient by $O_\epsilon(Q^\epsilon\phi(q_0)\sqrt{\operatorname{cond}(\nu_d)})$, before the optional replacement by $\sqrt d$. On $m$, there are two ordinary primitive-additive Gauss factors, each at most $\sqrt m$ including imprimitive characters. Thus the full individual character-pair coefficient is $O_\epsilon(Q^\epsilon\phi(q')\sqrt{\operatorname{cond}(\nu_d)})$, using $m/\phi(m)\ll_\epsilon Q^\epsilon$. In normalized residue coordinates there are two factors $\phi(q')^{-1}$ on expanding the legs. The sum over the one possible partner for each $\xi$ is bounded by $\phi(q')\|U\|_2\|V\|_2$ by Cauchy and finite Parseval. This yields (5) with no additional denominator norm factor. The singleton factors are treated in the same normalization.

For every product in the deleted sector, (4) and a power order witnessing (2) give

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\nu_d)\le 2V_Q D\,\operatorname{rad}(d).
 \tag{6}
$$

Thus (5) for such products costs at most $Q^\epsilon\sqrt{D\operatorname{rad}(d)}$ in the scalar bilinear norm. This is not a claim that the original conductor itself is $\le D$. In particular tame conductor loss under powering has not been omitted.

## 3. Fixed products, exact parity, and numerator counting

At fixed full product $\nu$, the old paired supports in $Z_{\rm sh}$ are fixed on the genuine progression leg: the earlier partial-product selections use $\rho$, with the corresponding unique partners, and the earlier R97 selection is conjugation-invariant on the alias leg. These old pair gates are implemented by the same projected vector on both raw legs at this $\nu$, as in R97 §2. The original high full-product mask keeps or omits an entire coefficient. The new test (2) also keeps or omits the entire coefficient, so does not change its raw vectors. The optional R86/R87 sharp parity pieces are treated through their actual per-leg projections. The existing character selections are fixed in the raw variables, the numerator, and the real shell parameter.

The genuine squared pointwise and integrated progression-increment bounds have their previous scale with upper fraction $1$ on the projected vectors. At a fixed full product, transposition for the two differences has sign $\nu(-1)$. Thus the exact common-test symmetric and skew arguments of [R93 §4](r93-operator-unequal-mixed-denominator-powers.md#4-fixed-aliases-parity-masks-and-full-packet-summation) apply using (5) to the true same vector and its raw Fourier transforms, respectively. The genuine sum orientation is unchanged. For the optional parity removals the two exact reflected derivative estimates still apply: residue reflection is diagonal in the leg characters, and product selection preserves their equality on the relevant auxiliary-even leg. This uses no marginal identity on the sharp masks. Both the original sign and overlap convention remain as before. With the original common-test seminorm $\mathcal K=K_1+QK'_2$, enlarged by $Q(E_\infty+E_1)$ in the reduced input as in R97 §2, the conditional HS bound at a removed full product is therefore

$$
O_\epsilon\!\left(Q^\epsilon\sqrt{D\operatorname{rad}(d)}
                                                \,\mathcal K\right).
 \tag{7}
$$

The enlargement uses $P/k\le1/2$ on the original optional parity pieces. This is the bound before any numerator-dependent detuning.

Let $T_u(d)=|\ker(\chi\mapsto\chi^u)|$ on the character group of $d$, with $T_u(1)=1$, and $H(d)=\sum_{1\le u\le V_Q}T_u(d)$. Denote the number of $\nu_d$'s satisfying (2) by $n_d$. Each power map has $T_u(d)$ elements in any attained fiber and there are at most $D\tau(d)$ image characters with primitive conductor $\le D$, as in [R90 §2](r90-operator-small-power-conductor-aliases.md#2-a-harmonic-average-of-character-power-fibers). In particular

$$
n_d\le D\tau(d)H(d),\qquad
           \frac{n_d}{\phi(d)}\ll_{\Theta,\epsilon}
                                   Q^\epsilon\frac{DH(d)}d .
 \tag{8}
$$

All additional product restrictions only decrease these counts. Allowing all $\rho\bmod m$ for this upper bound, there are at most $\phi(m)n_d$ relevant full product coefficients. The square of the joint numerator HS norm is $\phi(b)$ times their squared norm. Using $\phi(b)=\phi(d)\phi(m)$ and (7)–(8), the resulting per-actual-alias common-test cost is at most

$$
O_\epsilon\!\left(Q^\epsilon\, b D
                  \sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{rad}(d)H(d)}d}\,
                                      \mathcal K\right).
 \tag{9}
$$

The factors $D$ here come from the Gauss slope and its sparse product count, each under a square root. No phase or sign cancellations across different aliases are used.

## 4. Exact alias summation and the torsion-radical bound

At a fixed denominator $b$, group actual $k\le4M$ by their unitary divisor $d=b_\parallel$. Since $\operatorname{rad}(d)\mid 2k$, each group has $O(M/\operatorname{rad}(d))$ actual aliases, as in R91 (8). The group count decreases on every allowed subset; it is zero when the radical exceeds $8M$. Consequently the arithmetic factor in (9) at fixed $b$, after the real alias sum, is at most

$$
O_\epsilon\!\left(Q^\epsilon\,bMD
          \sum_{\substack{d\mid b,\ (d,b/d)=1\\d\ge D_0}}
                       \sqrt{\frac{H(d)}{d\operatorname{rad}(d)}}\right).
 \tag{10}
$$

We need the stronger radical-weight bound

$$
\sum_{d\le B}\frac{H(d)}{\operatorname{rad}(d)}
                       \ll_{\Theta,C,\epsilon}Q^\epsilon .
 \tag{11}
$$

For this purpose fix a positive $\delta$ and an integer $1\le u\le V_Q$. Rankin and multiplicativity majorize the sum for $T_u$ by

$$
B^\delta\prod_p
          \left(1+\frac1p\sum_{e\ge1}
                   \frac{T_u(p^e)}{p^{e\delta}}\right).
 \tag{12}
$$

At primes $p\mid2u$, use $T_u(p^e)\le2u$ and the geometric $e$ sum. Each local logarithm is $O_\delta(\log(2u))$, and their total is $O_\delta(\log^2(2u))$. At the other primes $T_u(p^e)=\gcd(u,p-1)$ is independent of $e$, and the local logarithms are bounded by $O_\delta(\gcd(u,p-1)/p^{1+\delta})$. There is a simple uniform bound for their sum: use the totient divisor identity for the gcd. For each $a\mid u$, primes $p\equiv1\bmod a$ are among $1+an$, $n\ge1$, so

$$
\sum_p\frac{\gcd(u,p-1)}{p^{1+\delta}}
       \le \sum_{a\mid u}\phi(a)
                \sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{(1+an)^{1+\delta}}
       \ll_\delta \sum_{a\mid u}\phi(a)a^{-1-\delta}
       \ll_\delta\tau(u).
$$

Thus the logarithm of the Euler product in (12) is $o(\log Q)$ uniformly for $u\le(\log Q)^C$, by the standard arbitrary-fixed-power divisor bound on $\tau(u)$. Take $\delta$ small relative to the desired $\epsilon$ and fixed polynomial upper bound on $B$. The $B^\delta$ cost, the uniform subpower factor and the sum over the polylogarithmic number of power orders fit (11). This Rankin argument permits arbitrary prime powers.

Square (10) by Cauchy over its at most $\tau(b)$ unitary divisors. Extend only the positive upper sum to all $dm\le B$. With $Z_b$ denoting the $b$-weighted sum inside that arithmetic factor without $Q^\epsilon MD$, this gives

$$
\begin{split}
   \sum_{b\le B} Z_b^2
      &\ll_\epsilon Q^\epsilon B^2
          \sum_{D_0\le d\le B}
                  \frac{H(d)}{d\,\operatorname{rad}(d)}\frac{B}{d}\\
      &\ll_{\Theta,C,\epsilon}
                  Q^\epsilon B^3/D_0^2 ,
 \end{split}
 \tag{13}
$$

by (11). Explicitly $Z_b=b\sum_{d\mid b,\ (d,b/d)=1,\ d\ge D_0}\sqrt{H(d)/(d\operatorname{rad}(d))}$. The power of $D_0$ in this estimate comes from the lower gate on $d$; the enlarged count is never an enlargement of the actual deletion.

Apply the exact [R77 (7)](r77-operator-common-shell-rational-packets.md#3-the-true-central-tapered-field) common Taylor tests with each original scalar detuning coefficient bounded by $2^{-n}$ at Taylor order $n$. At offset $\ell$, the weighted common $K_1$ is $O(1+|\ell|)$; the other weighted seminorms cost $O(S^{-1}+(1+|\ell|)^2/Q)$, as in R97. Keep the original unit-modulus phases, divide by $J\asymp Q$ and square-sum over $|\ell|\le R+1$. Applying (13) to (10) proves (3). The arithmetic prefactor at the stated scales is $O(Q^{988/1000})$ before the offset bracket. The three final powers are $-33/4000,-23/4000,-27/4000$, so the earlier sharp comparison persists by triangle. No centering, edge, or deterministic term has been recomputed.

## 5. Strict eligibility without a small alias conductor

At the numerical parameters let $d=2^e$ be the least power of $2$ at least $Q^{1/5}$, choose an odd prime $m\asymp Q^{19/100}$ with $b=dm$ in the high band, and take $k=r\asymp Q^{9/20}$ an odd prime. Bertrand's postulate gives such choices. Thus $r_b=r$, $(2k,b)=2<G$, and $d\ge D_0,\ k>K$. On $r$ take a generator-order character and its conjugate. On $m$ choose a generator $\eta$, and set $\eta'=\eta^t$ for $t=2V^*$, with $V^*$ at least all the finitely many relevant polylogarithmic power cutoffs. Every allowed signed mixed power on $m$ has nonzero exponent $u+tv$ of absolute size $<m-1$ for large $Q$. The individual and product powers are also nonprincipal. Consequently the relevant $m$-conductor remains $m$, and $rm\asymp Q^{16/25}>Q^{3/5}$, excluding the earlier signed joint deletion. The earlier alias powers all retain conductor $r$.

On $2^{e+1}$ use a conjugate primitive pair. Its shared product $\nu_d$ is principal and its exact local incidence is nonzero with magnitude $2^{e+1}$ by [R94 §5](r94-operator-ungated-mixed-denominator-powers.md#5-a-nonzero-incidence-above-the-former-excess-gate). The full product and all its earlier permitted powers remain high from the $m$-component. The coprime $m$ Gauss factors and alias conjugacy factor are also nonzero. For the R97 shared joint test the alias conductor is $r>Q^{51/125}$, so no earlier allowed alias power can meet that test even with principal $\nu_d$. Yet (2) holds for the present field. This is strict eligibility of an actual nonzero original incidence coefficient in the unmodified input, not a lower bound on the integrated field.
