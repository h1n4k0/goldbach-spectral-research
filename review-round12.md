# Independent audit of the two Round 12 proofs

Checked [adaptive fixed-block proof](spectral-post-r11-adaptive.md), SHA-256 `5d2f34c82924785cad74f2238fe75f4b2047efd8450cd566f8e4a746cf99ebd8`, and [growing-prefix proof](arithmetic-round12-prefix.md), SHA-256 `b5c8dc51af59fdb9a8b57028975980cc4dbd54d6e00d77e74b8c341b355fbc10`, against [pinned Round 11](https://github.com/h1n4k0/goldbach-spectral-research/blob/95818f1d66a3a937f6e17a1b8b046a0fde3c64c1/arithmetic-post-round10-exceptions.md). Both proofs pass internal review. Results of ongoing experiments are not part of this checkpoint.

* **Shared adaptive argument.** The individual sum or difference line determines the second summand, reducing two adaptive tests to one interval. Decompose only that interval into disjoint dyadic blocks. At a fixed level each selected index occurs at most twice; use Hölder $(2,4,4)$, then Cauchy over blocks. Disjointness yields the $L^2$ Parseval bound for the adaptive factor; only the fixed full prime sum needs a minor-arc $L^4$ bound. The sparse fourth norm follows by factoring nonzero differences of squares, or $F(r)-F(u)=(r-u)(a_2(r+u)+a_1)$ for any fixed quadratic, with the zero difference counted separately. Difference aliases require the conjugate of the full prime sum; absolute estimates are unchanged. Major arcs are evaluated individually before summing over moduli, with uniform endpoint and Ramanujan-tail bounds.
* **Fixed-block consequence.** The layer cake gives a linear bound on each Hilbert–Schmidt norm by its own supremum of unweighted signed rectangles. It is legitimate to select the interval separately for every modulus, since the adaptive proof precedes the selection. Substitution of $P=\lfloor X^{1/3}\tau^{2/3}L^{-10}\rfloor$ gives two major errors $O(\tau L^{-13}+\tau^2L^{-25})$; the individual series tail is $o(\tau)$ in the stated range. The exception calculation gives $X^{1/3+\epsilon}\tau^{-7/3}$. Integrating its distribution at the split $\tau_0=X^{-1/14}$ gives the $X^{-1/7+\epsilon}$ squared mean, using a separate elementary uniform upper bound for the part above $1$.
* **Growing-prefix consequence.** Normalize every line by $1/N$, retain all sum and signed difference aliases with $k\le2b$, and use common raw scale $Z\asymp bX$. In the bulk the selected $L^1$ log-kernel factor $1/\sqrt{xy}$ cancels against the individual short-axis sieve bound with factor $\sqrt{xy}$. Thus the log area costs only $O(L^2)$. On the true edge $x<X^{-1/4}$, a contributing line forces $y\ge3/4$; the Hardy reciprocal weight is integrable in $dx$ even below $1/N$, giving squared error $O(bL^4X^{-1/4})$ per modulus. Both this and the major error are absorbable with $b^2\tau^{-2}\le X^{\delta/2}$. Squaring the remaining selected-support factor gives exactly $\tau^{-4}b^{13/3+\delta/2}X^{1/3+\delta/2+\epsilon}$; the example has exponent $0.416966\ldots+\epsilon$.

These are distinct fixed-block and growing-prefix benefits of shared inputs rather than two independent minor-arc methods. The arithmetic estimate assumes global Dirichlet GRH for auxiliary conductors; the Hardy identification also uses the corresponding local GRH(N). The arguments permit remaining exceptions and do not control exterior crossings, the full spectral norm, or either S/R hypothesis.
