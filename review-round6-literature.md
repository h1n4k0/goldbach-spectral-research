# Fixed-modulus inputs: what would close the remaining norm bounds?

This is a bounded literature and quantifier audit, not a claim of a new estimate for the true residual. It uses [the full-space compression](operator-round4.md), [the near/far splitting](operator-round5.md), and the exact signed real-variable pairing of [source-round2.md](source-round2.md). We did not find an established fixed-modulus theorem capable of providing either missing uniform bound at the relevant rate.

## What to ask a theorem to deliver

Fix $A>0$, put $L=\log N$, and write $\varepsilon_N=\mathfrak S(N)L^{-A}$, stronger than the unspecified-rate existential S. Take the checked log-coordinate compression to normalized indicators of cells of length $d=L^{-C_a}$ over $[0,Y]$, with $Y=C_A L\log L$ and sufficiently large fixed constants to make compression error $o(\varepsilon_N)$. Use the smooth log-band mask of round 5 with $B=C_{A,\kappa}\log L$. Here log coordinate means **log of the raw additive size**, not zero height. Let $M_{\rm near}$ be the actual near residual matrix and $M_{\rm far}^Z$ its far zero-residue proxy.

* **A sufficient near-input theorem:** uniformly for each growing even $N$ satisfying local GRH, bound the full signed odd-character discrepancy for these cells, including the two difference congruences **minus the sum/reflection congruence**, subtracting the actual diagonal and the Beta mean. A concrete entrywise sufficient rate is $o(\varepsilon_N d/B)$ for every surviving masked entry. Indeed each row has $O(1+B/d)$ entries, so Schur suffices; a direct operator estimate $o(\varepsilon_N)$ could be strictly easier. An all-character AP variance cannot substitute for the odd difference: for real residue data $A_r$, the odd quadratic form is proportional to $\sum_r|A_r-A_{-r}|^2$, not $\sum_r|A_r-\bar A|^2$. One elementary check on any proposed theorem is to test a fixed symmetric bump in $u=n/N\in(1/3,2/3)$. Its log embedding occupies a fixed-width band, and its signed form contains the balanced Goldbach main-term subtraction even when one examines that single direction.
* **A sufficient far-input theorem:** uniformly for the same cells restricted to separations at least $B-2d$, estimate the **two-argument, parity-restricted** covariance between the weighted zero residue sum in the high cell and the Dirichlet logarithmic-derivative vector in the low cell. A concrete entrywise sufficient rate is $o(\varepsilon_N d/Y)$, or use absolute row sums $o(\varepsilon_N)$, or bound the actual masked covariance as an operator. The pole-free diagonal/Beta terms are already negligible there. A diagonal second moment controls far covariance only by Cauchy at the main-term scale; it provides no phase cancellation. Even a scalar all-fixed-weight asymptotic that one can polarize has to provide quantitative uniformity as the cells narrow and their centers move, and genuine joint information across widely separated windows. A fixed-window pair law is not such a theorem.

These entrywise conditions are **sufficient, not necessary**; no lower bound on the true norm follows from not knowing them. The full near/far operator norms, rather than these conservative Schur rates, are the equivalence at this chosen rate. For R one must choose the substantially larger log truncation and finer resolution appropriate to its $N^{-1/2}$-type threshold, and leave margin for the exact leading constant.

## Closest primary results and why they do not transfer

1. [Travor Zihao Liu, *Pair correlation of L-zeros and Hooley's conjecture*, Hexagon preprint v1, Nov. 16, 2026, Theorems 1–4](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00509v1) is particularly close. For **each odd prime** modulus, its same-character form factor averages over **all primitive** characters, not just odd ones. It obtains the scalar ramp below $|\alpha|=1$ unconditionally; for $1\le|\alpha|\le D$ (fixed $D$), Theorem 3 identifies the scalar plateau with the corresponding smooth Hooley variance asymptotic over $x^{1/D}\le q\le x$. This is an equivalence with a conjectural input, not an established plateau there. The extension below $2$ in Theorem 4 assumes GRH and a uniform smooth Hardy–Littlewood conjecture for translated prime pairs. It reports leading relative accuracy for fixed profiles, not the absolute growing-matrix accuracy above. The parity-reflection terms and fixed composite even $N$ do not come from that result. Also the theorem's $\alpha=\log x/\log q$ bounded by fixed $D$ does not cover the whole Hardy log band $Y/L\asymp\log L$, or the $Y/L\asymp L$ range relevant to R. Reuse of its explicit-formula/variance comparison may illuminate individual tests, but cannot by itself close either block. This preprint is **not** the arXiv paper with a coincident numeric identifier.
2. [Chandee–Lee–Liu–Radziwiłł, *Simple zeros of primitive Dirichlet L-functions and the asymptotic large sieve*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6725) has an extended-support result under GRH using a smooth **average over moduli**. It is not an estimate for each even $N$, and averaging cannot remove rare exceptions. [Yıldırım's 1991 Theorem, as stated precisely in Kandhil–Languasco–Moree §1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-026-03383-y), is a **different** two-character sum weighted by a residue class and a zero-height smoothing; its proven range requires $T\ge (x/q)\log x$, $q\le \sqrt{x}/(\log x)^3$. It does not estimate the present fixed-height, same-character cross kernel. Neither can be substituted for the sought per-modulus arithmetic bound.
3. [Fiorilli–Martin, *Disproving Hooley's conjecture*](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/1291) is tempting as a source of a refutation, but its large variance at $q\asymp\log\log x$ occurs at vastly greater raw additive sizes than these norm-relevant ranges: on the R log band $\log x=O((\log N)^2)$, hence $\log\log x=O(\log\log N)$, not $\asymp N$. Full far-tail norm control already excludes this scale as a direct obstruction. Its statistic also does not isolate odd parity. No contradiction to S/R follows.

**Handoff.** A usable analytic input would have to retain odd reflection and fixed modulus while proving quantitative cancellation at a growing family of tests; a true fixed-modulus signed prime-pair estimate at the stated near Schur rate, or a two-scale odd covariance estimate at the stated far rate, would deliver a specific block bound. The closest primary result retains fixed prime modulus but loses parity, testing range, and rate; the available asymptotic large sieve gains testing range by surrendering the fixed modulus. No established input met the full specification. An additional unsupported S/R prediction, an extra scalar reformulation, or a generic literature sweep would not advance this step.
