This is an internal proof check, not external peer review.

- **On each actual signed line**, the long coordinate is determined by the short coordinate. Its original hard interval can be pulled back before replacing the long exponential-sum factor by the common full interval. Both the discrete sum and the completed continuum overlap are therefore preserved. The scale is $1/N$, not $1/(kN)$, and $N\asymp X$, with all $O(Y)$ aliases inside the stated raw bounds. Conjugation covers both difference orientations. Equal prime pairs and proper powers are covered by the stated per-line unit-restoration bound.
- **Unequal major-arc scales.** On $H=P/(qZ)$, Abel endpoints and real-line Plancherel give the squared character error $O(L^4(UH+U^2H^2))$ for either $U=W$ or $U=Z$. Finite Parseval (all numerators) and summation give $O((U/Z)P^2L^5)$, including the nonunit part. The short principal squared mass is $O(WL)$, so both mixed products genuinely cost $O(\sqrt W P L^3)$, even the one with the long principal factor. The double error is no larger than $O(\sqrt W P L^5)$ since $P<\sqrt Z$. The Ramanujan and completed integral tails are individual, not on average: using both interval transforms $O(1/|\beta|)$ outside each arc suffices. The proof uses **global Dirichlet GRH** for this argument.
- **Unequal minor-arc scales.** Per-level short dyadic interval energies sum to $O(WL)$, while the long factor is fixed and has the classical Vaughan fourth-norm bound at $P=Z^{2/5}$. The arbitrary signed selected fourth moment retains its divisor bound after multiplying frequencies by a fixed $k$. Hölder and Cauchy produce $\sqrt{MW} Z^{3/4}P^{-1/4}X^\epsilon$ for one alias. No extra fourth-moment preprint is used.
- **True Hilbert space.** The physical kernel acts on Lebesgue $dx$; the factors $2\pi (xy)^{-3/2}/\sqrt{w_N(x)w_N(y)}$ are exactly those of the previously verified Volterra identification. After the layer-cake bound, the selected bulk kernel is $O(C_E/(xy))$ in sum of absolute values, and the individual shorter-axis sieve cap is $O(\mathfrak S(N))$. The $dx\,dy$ integration costs two logarithms. Nonzero kernel on either low edge forces the opposite coordinate to be at least $3/4$. Below $1/N$ the arithmetic part vanishes, and the model's square is bounded by a constant times $(N\min(x,y))^{-1/4}$; it is integrable. Thus the edge contributes $O(YL^4X^{-1/4})$ per tested modulus. No modes have been projected out inside the indicated rectangle.
- **Relative sizes and scope.** At $B=X^{1/100}$, $Y=X^{1/20}$, $\tau=X^{-1/400}$, the major mixed, completed series, unit-restoration and edge coefficients have exponents $-0.025$, $-0.32+o(1)$, $-0.425+o(1)$ and $-0.2+o(1)$, all smaller than the threshold-squared exponent $-0.005$. The selected minor term then gives the claimed exception exponent $0.30+0.01+3.3(0.05)+0.01=97/200$. This extends classical conditional coverage to an explicitly larger finite cross range, not a smaller exceptional count on the original window. It bounds $P_B T_N P_Y$ and its adjoint, not the high–high component within $P_Y$, either crossing past $Y$, a singleton modulus, or S/R.
